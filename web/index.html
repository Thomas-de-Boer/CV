Watch a neural network read your handwriting

Draw a digit. A small neural network, written from scratch in Rust and running right here in your browser, lights up layer by layer as it decides what you drew.

An experiment to see how good AI is at making AI. Nobody trained this version. Claude, an AI model, chose every one of the 25,818 weights itself by reasoning about what handwritten digits look like, with no training data and without ever correcting a mistake. It has never seen a real handwritten digit.

On 10,000 real handwritten digits it gets 39.7% right. Pure chance would be 10%; the trained version gets 96.8%.

Learned from 70,000 handwritten digits. This version started with completely random weights, so at first it was right only by luck. It was then shown every image in the MNIST collection of handwritten digits, 70,000 of them, 20 times over. For each image it made its guess, the guess was compared with the right answer, and every weight was nudged a tiny bit in the direction that would have made the answer more right (backpropagation). That happened in small groups of 32 images: 43,760 rounds of nudging all 25,818 weights, about 25 seconds on a laptop.

Trained the same way on 60,000 of the images, it reads 96.8% of the other 10,000 correctly, images it had never seen. The version running here then used those 10,000 as well.
